\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Scientific Limitations}
\label{subsec:scientific_limitations}
The Kaggle Disease Prediction dataset is a rule-derived benchmark rather than a prospective clinical patient cohort. The absence of patient demographics (age, sex, vitals, medical history, comorbidities) represents an inherent structural constraint. Consequently, this system is strictly evaluated as an algorithmic decision-support prototype under simulated benchmark stress testing. Statistical stability across feature selection algorithms and cross-validation folds establishes dataset-level algorithmic evidence, but does not substitute for independent external clinical validation.

\subsection{Ethical Framework and Clinical Safety}
\label{subsec:ethical_framework}
The decision-support prototype is engineered with explicit safety constraints: it outputs candidate probabilities, supporting/opposing evidence, and a mandatory disclaimer advising formal clinical consultation. The system is explicitly designed as an assistive triage aid, not an autonomous diagnostic agent.

\section{Stochastic Reporting Noise and Error Asymmetry (Hypothesis H3)}
\label{sec:reporting_noise}

In ambulatory and emergency intake settings, patient symptom reporting is intrinsically bidirectional and imperfect: individuals may omit active clinical signs (false negatives / symptom dropout) or erroneously endorse unassociated manifestations (false positives / spurious activations). To stress-test algorithmic resilience under real-world reporting noise, we evaluated all six representative model architectures across 35 experimental conditions using Duplicate-Aware Group 10-Fold Cross-Validation (Protocol C) and 5 independent stochastic perturbation seeds ($N = 20,520$ fold-level evaluations).

\subsection{Two-Tier Evaluation of Hypothesis H3}
\label{subsec:h3_evaluation}

Hypothesis H3 posits that \textit{spurious symptom activations (false positives) will cause steeper diagnostic performance drops than symptom omissions (false negatives)}. Our empirical benchmark evaluates this phenomenon across two complementary tiers:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Primary Rate Grid ($p_{\text{drop}} \in [0.05, 0.30]$ vs.\ $p_{\text{fp}} \in [0.01, 0.10]$)}: Under raw percentage rates, false-positive activation causes rapid diagnostic failure in models with partitioning logic. For instance, in Random Forest, Macro-F1 drops moderately from $0.987 \pm 0.022$ at baseline to $0.911 \pm 0.030$ under a 20\% dropout rate. In contrast, under a modest 5\% false-positive activation rate, Random Forest Macro-F1 collapses precipitously to $0.467 \pm 0.038$ (Top-1 accuracy collapsing from $0.994$ to $0.550$). This divergence stems from feature space sparsity: activating 5\% of the 124.55 typically absent symptoms introduces $\sim 6.23$ spurious positive features, overpowering the true clinical signal of the $7.45$ present symptoms.
    
    \item \textbf{Matched-Error-Burden Control (Equal Feature Mutations)}: To rule out denominator asymmetry and evaluate intrinsic error severity, we conducted matched-error-burden evaluations where the false-positive rate was calibrated ($p_{\text{fp}} = 0.0120$) to induce the exact expected number of altered features ($\sim 1.49$ symptoms) as a 20\% dropout rate ($p_{\text{drop}} = 0.20$):
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Under 20\% Dropout (1.49 omitted symptoms): Logistic Regression retains Macro-F1 of $0.942 \pm 0.015$, and Random Forest retains $0.911 \pm 0.030$.
        \item Under Matched 1.20\% False Positives (1.49 spurious symptoms): Logistic Regression retains $0.999 \pm 0.003$ due to linear soft-margin compensation, whereas Random Forest collapses to $0.774 \pm 0.035$ (Top-1 accuracy $0.931$).
    \end{itemize}
    Crucially, the empirical effect of symptom-reporting noise is model-dependent. False-positive activations cause substantially greater degradation in Random Forest and XGBoost under the matched-error-burden comparison, whereas several other models (such as Logistic Regression and MLP) exhibit greater degradation under the tested dropout condition. Hypothesis H3 is therefore only partially supported and should not be generalized to all model architectures.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Architectural Susceptibility and the Differential Safeguard}
\label{subsec:noise_architectures}

As detailed in \cref{tab:noise_summary}, architectural resilience to reporting noise varies substantially across model paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tree Ensembles (Random Forest and XGBoost)}: Partitioning algorithms prove highly vulnerable to false-positive activations. Spurious positive indicators satisfy split predicates high in the decision trees, misrouting patient instances into incorrect terminal leaves.
    \item \textbf{Additive and Linear Models (Logistic Regression, MLP, SVM)}: Additive architectures demonstrate superior robustness to low-level spurious noise because individual false-positive features contribute bounded additive log-odds that can be outvoted by the constellation of remaining true positive symptoms.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Candidate Differential Resilience}: Crucially, even when single-label Top-1 accuracy drops under combined noise (`comb\_d10\_fp2`), \textbf{Top-3 and Top-5 diagnostic inclusion remains exceptionally resilient} ($\ge 97.6\%$ for Random Forest and $\ge 99.4\%$ for MLP and SVM), reaffirming that differential candidate ranking provides an essential buffer against catastrophic misdiagnosis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Probability Calibration and Uncertainty Quantification (Hypothesis H4)}
\label{sec:calibration}

In clinical triage, accurate candidate disease ranking must be paired with statistically reliable posterior probability estimates. A decision support system that outputs a 90\% probability for Dengue should ideally be correct 90\% of the time. Uncalibrated modern models, especially non-linear neural networks and tree ensembles, frequently suffer from overconfidence or skewed probability distributions~\cite{guo2017calibration}.

To address this challenge, we conducted a systematic empirical evaluation of post-hoc probability calibration across all representative model architectures under Duplicate-Aware Group 10-Fold Cross-Validation (Protocol C) with zero inner-to-outer validation leakage ($N = 2,700$ fold-level evaluations). We compared uncalibrated native models against two primary parametric and non-parametric scaling techniques: Platt / Sigmoid calibration~\cite{platt1999probabilistic} and Isotonic regression~\cite{zadrozny2002transforming}, evaluated across multi-class log loss and strictly proper Brier scores~\cite{brier1950verification, niculescumizil2005predicting}.

\end{figure}

\subsection{Evaluation of Hypothesis H4}
\label{subsec:h4_evaluation}

Hypothesis H4 posits that \textit{post-hoc probability calibration (Platt scaling and Isotonic regression) will significantly reduce Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier scores across tree and neural classifiers without degrading classification accuracy}.

As shown in \cref{tab:calibration_summary} and \cref{fig:calibration_metrics}, empirical cross-validation reveals that **Hypothesis H4 is partially supported (architecture-dependent)**:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Random Forest}: Native uncalibrated Random Forest exhibits an ECE of $0.1114 \pm 0.0218$ and a multi-class Brier score of $0.0581$. Non-parametric Isotonic regression successfully reduces ECE by almost half to $\mathbf{0.0575 \pm 0.0278}$ (a $-48.4\%$ reduction), while driving the Brier score down to $0.0155$ and multiclass log loss from $0.1524 \to 0.0647$, all while preserving $100\%$ Top-1 accuracy.
    \item \textbf{MLP Neural Network}: Native uncalibrated MLP displays significant overconfidence with a high native ECE of $0.2680 \pm 0.0752$ and Brier score of $0.1294$. Isotonic calibration achieves an exceptional **80.7\% ECE reduction**, bringing ECE down to $\mathbf{0.0517 \pm 0.0275}$ and Brier score to $0.0157$ ($100\%$ accuracy preserved). Platt scaling also achieves strong calibration (ECE $0.0869$, Brier $0.0143$).
    \item \textbf{Extra Trees}: Isotonic regression reduces ECE from $0.1042 \pm 0.0327$ to $\mathbf{0.0629 \pm 0.0283}$ and Brier score from $0.0838 \to 0.0186$.
    \item \textbf{Single Decision Tree Anomaly}: In contrast to ensembles, single unpruned decision trees do not benefit from calibration under Protocol C; Isotonic regression overfits the validation split, increasing ECE from $0.1743 \to 0.3304$.
    \item \textbf{Linear and Naive Bayes Models}: Regularized Logistic Regression and Naive Bayes already possess well-calibrated native posterior probabilities on this benchmark (native ECE $0.0282$ and $0.0084$, respectively), rendering post-hoc calibration redundant.
\end{itemize}

\section{Explainable AI and Clinical Literature Concordance (Hypothesis H5)}
\label{sec:explainable_ai}

In clinical machine learning, transparency is an imperative for physician trust and algorithmic causability~\cite{holzinger2019causability, molnar2020interpretable}. As argued by Rudin~\cite{rudin2019stop}, automated decision-support outputs must provide interpretable representations rather than opaque black-box recommendations. We employed SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)~\cite{lundberg2017unified}—specifically TreeSHAP for tree ensembles and LinearSHAP for regularized linear models—to quantify the marginal contribution of each symptom to the model's posterior log-odds.

\subsection{Cross-Fold Attribution Stability}
\label{subsec:shap_stability}

A key requirement for clinical explainability is that explanations must not fluctuate wildly across different training splits. We quantified cross-fold explanation stability across all Protocol C folds using pairwise Jaccard similarity of the top-5 most important symptoms and Spearman rank correlation of symptom weights:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tree Ensembles (Random Forest and XGBoost)}: Exhibit high attribution stability across folds, achieving mean Top-5 Jaccard similarities of $0.5265 \pm 0.2500$ (Random Forest) and $0.6435 \pm 0.2984$ (XGBoost), with positive Spearman rank correlations ($0.2981$ and $0.2833$).
    \item \textbf{Single Decision Tree Instability}: In stark contrast, single unpruned decision trees exhibit extreme explanation instability, with a mean Jaccard similarity of only $0.1030 \pm 0.1273$ and an inverted Spearman rank correlation ($-0.6033$), proving that greedy single-tree splits produce idiosyncratic, ungeneralizable explanations.
    \item \textbf{Perturbation Stability Under Sparsity and Noise}: When evaluated under test-time perturbations, Random Forest explanations proved highly resilient, retaining a Jaccard stability of $0.9048$ under severe symptom sparsity ($s=3$) and $0.9593$ under 20\% symptom dropout noise.
\end{itemize}

\section{Clinical Decision Support System Research Prototype}
\label{sec:prototype}

To bridge empirical research and practical utility, we developed an interactive Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) research prototype implementing the validated models, probability calibrator, and explainability engine, adhering to clinical AI design paradigms articulated by Shortliffe and Sepúlveda~\cite{shortliffe2018clinical}, Topol~\cite{topol2019high}, and Fraser et al.~\cite{fraser2018evaluating}.

\subsection{System Architecture and Implementation}
\label{subsec:prototype_arch}

The prototype is engineered as a decoupled, responsive microservice architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Backend Service (FastAPI / Python)}: A high-performance asynchronous REST API running on port `8181`. The backend service encapsulates inference logic, feature mapping, and safety verification. At startup, the service initializes pre-compiled research artifacts generated from our Protocol C pipeline:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item `model_pipeline.joblib`: The primary Random Forest multiclass classifier.
        \item `calibrator.joblib`: The fitted Isotonic regression calibrator mapping raw confidence scores to calibrated posterior probabilities.
        \item `explainer.joblib`: The TreeSHAP explainer for computing real-time local patient feature contributions.
        \item `model_metadata.json`: The canonical vocabulary of 132 standardized symptoms and 41 disease classes.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frontend Application (Vanilla Web Architecture)}: A modern, lightweight Single Page Application (SPA) utilizing CSS Glassmorphism and modern responsive layout principles. The user interface allows clinicians or researchers to select presenting symptoms via real-time search, categorized chips, and chief complaint buffers.
    \item \textbf{API Endpoints}: The system exposes modular endpoints:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item `GET /health`: Pipeline readiness, calibrator status, and feature/class verification.
        \item `GET /catalogue/symptoms` \& `GET /catalogue/diseases`: Full ontology catalogues.
        \item `POST /predict`: Generates Top-$k$ candidate differential diagnosis with calibrated probabilities.
        \item `POST /explain`: Generates local directional SHAP attribution waterfalls (supporting vs.\ opposing symptoms).
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Responsible Decision-Support Presentation Paradigm}
\label{subsec:prototype_presentation}

The prototype adheres strictly to ethical clinical decision-support design:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Differential Diagnostic Candidates}: Instead of issuing brittle single-label claims (*"You have Disease X"*), the interface presents a stratified differential candidate list ($Top-k \in \{3, 5, 10\}$) displaying calibrated probability estimates and confidence tiers (High, Moderate, Low).
    \item \textbf{Directional Symptom Evidence}: For each candidate condition, the system visualizes positive supporting evidence ($+$ SHAP) and absent/opposing symptoms ($-$ SHAP), allowing the clinician to immediately evaluate the biological rationale behind the recommendation.
    \item \textbf{Safety Guardrails}: The interface renders a mandatory clinical advisory notice explicitly designating the application as a non-autonomous research and triage aid that requires assessment by a licensed healthcare professional.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Verification and Performance Latency}
\label{subsec:prototype_verification}

The prototype implementation was validated using an automated test suite comprising **22 unit and integration tests** (`tests/test_api.py`) executed under `pytest`:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Verification of strict Pydantic request/response schema validation and unknown symptom filtering.
    \item Mathematical bounds testing confirming that candidate posterior probabilities satisfy $\sum_{c} \hat{p}_c = 1.0$ and $\hat{p}_c \in [0, 1]$.
    \item End-to-end response latency benchmarks demonstrating a **mean end-to-end inference latency under 15 milliseconds**, satisfying real-time triage requirements.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This research establishes a comprehensive, leakage-aware foundation for symptom-based disease prediction:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Forensic Benchmark Audit}: We forensically exposed the 93.8\% synthetic duplication and 97.6\% train-test leakage in the widely cited Kaggle benchmark, demonstrating why prevailing na\"{i}ve 100\% accuracy claims are methodologically flawed.
    \item \textbf{Validation Protocol Hierarchy}: Through leakage-aware Duplicate-Aware Group Cross-Validation (Protocol C), we proved that partitioned tree-based models suffer substantial degradation (Decision Tree: $-17.44\%$, XGBoost: $-12.80\%$) on unseen symptom profiles, confirming Hypothesis H1.
    \item \textbf{Leakage-Aware Feature Selection}: We established that a compact ontology of 50 consensus symptoms preserves $\ge 95\%$ retained Macro-F1 on the benchmark, identifying an algorithmically stable symptom subspace.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Symptom Sparsity}: Under controlled symptom sparsity simulations, while single-label Top-1 accuracy declines as inputs are restricted to acute chief complaints ($s \le 3$), Top-3 and Top-5 candidate ranking retains over 88\% and 93\% inclusion, confirming Hypothesis H2.
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Reporting Noise Benchmark}: Through 20,520 cross-validation runs, we proved that noise sensitivity is model-dependent under matched-error-burden controls, demonstrating that spurious activations severely degrade tree architectures, partially supporting Hypothesis H3.
    \item \textbf{Probability Calibration}: Across 2,700 evaluation runs, post-hoc Isotonic regression reduced Expected Calibration Error by up to 80\% in neural networks and 48\% in tree ensembles without degrading accuracy, partially supporting Hypothesis H4.
    \item \textbf{Explainability and Prototype Deployment}: We demonstrated a 72.7\% concordance between TreeSHAP attributions and published WHO/CDC guidelines (supporting Hypothesis H5) and translated these findings into a fully operational, test-validated CDSS research prototype.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key Findings Summary}
\label{sec:key_findings}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The Kaggle Disease Prediction dataset contains only 304 unique symptom vectors across 4,920 records (93.8\% duplication), with 41 of 42 test records being exact duplicates of training records.
    \item Duplicate-Aware Group CV (Protocol C) exposes that Decision Tree accuracy collapses by $-17.44\%$ and XGBoost by $-12.80\%$ relative to standard cross-validation.
    \item A compact ontology of 50 consensus symptoms satisfies the $\ge 95\%$ retained Macro-F1 operational criterion at the dataset level.
    \item Multi-candidate differential ranking (Top-3 and Top-5) provides a robust safeguard against single-label accuracy collapse under both feature reduction and simulated symptom sparsity.
    \item Reporting error sensitivity is model-dependent: false-positive activations cause substantially steeper degradation in tree ensembles (Random Forest F1 collapses to $0.774$ under matched false positives vs.\ $0.911$ under matching dropout).
    \item Post-hoc Isotonic calibration significantly improves posterior probability reliability, reducing Random Forest ECE from $0.1114 \to 0.0575$ and MLP ECE from $0.2680 \to 0.0517$.
    \item TreeSHAP attributions exhibit strong cross-fold stability (Random Forest Jaccard $0.5265$) and 72.7\% agreement with authoritative medical diagnostic literature (WHO, CDC, ADA, ACG, GINA).
    \item The framework is fully realized in a responsive, test-validated CDSS research prototype featuring differential candidate ranking and directional evidence visualization.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work and Research Roadmap}
\label{sec:future_work}

The findings of this thesis establish a rigorous algorithmic baseline for subsequent research extensions:

\subsection{Prospective Multi-Center Clinical Cohort Validation}
\label{subsec:real_world_validation}
The foremost priority is validating the algorithmic framework on prospectively collected clinical patient cohorts featuring authentic demographic diversity, verified electronic health records (EHR), and complex real-world comorbidities, testing generalizability beyond synthetic rule-derived benchmarks.

\subsection{Multi-Modal Diagnostic Integration}
\label{subsec:multimodal_integration}
Future iterations should extend beyond subjective patient-reported symptom vectors by fusing continuous physiological telemetry, point-of-care vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation), and objective laboratory diagnostic panels (complete blood count, metabolic profiles).

\subsection{Dynamic Longitudinal Disease Trajectory Modeling}
\label{subsec:longitudinal_modeling}
Incorporating temporal dynamics—such as symptom onset velocity, duration, and progressive severity grading—into sequential neural models (e.g., Recurrent Neural Networks or Transformers) to capture evolving disease presentations over time.
