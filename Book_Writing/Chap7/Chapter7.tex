\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Scientific Limitations}
\label{subsec:scientific_limitations}
The Kaggle Disease Prediction dataset is a rule-derived benchmark rather than a prospective clinical patient cohort. The absence of patient demographics (age, sex, vitals, medical history, comorbidities) represents an inherent structural constraint. Consequently, this system is strictly evaluated as an algorithmic decision-support prototype under simulated benchmark stress testing. Statistical stability across feature selection algorithms and cross-validation folds establishes dataset-level algorithmic evidence, but does not substitute for independent external clinical validation.

\subsection{Ethical Framework and Clinical Safety}
\label{subsec:ethical_framework}
The decision-support prototype is engineered with explicit safety constraints: it outputs candidate probabilities, supporting/opposing evidence, and a mandatory disclaimer advising formal clinical consultation. The system is explicitly designed as an assistive triage aid, not an autonomous diagnostic agent.

\section{Stochastic Reporting Noise and Error Asymmetry (Hypothesis H3)}
\label{sec:reporting_noise}

In ambulatory and emergency intake settings, patient symptom reporting is intrinsically bidirectional and imperfect: individuals may omit active clinical signs (false negatives / symptom dropout) or erroneously endorse unassociated manifestations (false positives / spurious activations). To stress-test algorithmic resilience under real-world reporting noise, we evaluated all six representative model architectures across 35 experimental conditions using Duplicate-Aware Group 10-Fold Cross-Validation (Protocol C) and 5 independent stochastic perturbation seeds ($N = 20,520$ fold-level evaluations).

\subsection{Two-Tier Evaluation of Hypothesis H3}
\label{subsec:h3_evaluation}

Hypothesis H3 posits that \textit{spurious symptom activations (false positives) will cause steeper diagnostic performance drops than symptom omissions (false negatives)}. Our empirical benchmark evaluates this phenomenon across two complementary tiers:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Primary Rate Grid ($p_{\text{drop}} \in [0.05, 0.30]$ vs.\ $p_{\text{fp}} \in [0.01, 0.10]$)}: Under raw percentage rates, false-positive activation causes rapid diagnostic failure in models with partitioning logic. For instance, in Random Forest, Macro-F1 drops moderately from $0.987 \pm 0.022$ at baseline to $0.911 \pm 0.030$ under a 20\% dropout rate. In contrast, under a modest 5\% false-positive activation rate, Random Forest Macro-F1 collapses precipitously to $0.467 \pm 0.038$ (Top-1 accuracy collapsing from $0.994$ to $0.550$). This divergence stems from feature space sparsity: activating 5\% of the 124.55 typically absent symptoms introduces $\sim 6.23$ spurious positive features, overpowering the true clinical signal of the $7.45$ present symptoms.
    
    \item \textbf{Matched-Error-Burden Control (Equal Feature Mutations)}: To rule out denominator asymmetry and evaluate intrinsic error severity, we conducted matched-error-burden evaluations where the false-positive rate was calibrated ($p_{\text{fp}} = 0.0120$) to induce the exact expected number of altered features ($\sim 1.49$ symptoms) as a 20\% dropout rate ($p_{\text{drop}} = 0.20$):
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Under 20\% Dropout (1.49 omitted symptoms): Logistic Regression retains Macro-F1 of $0.942 \pm 0.015$, and Random Forest retains $0.911 \pm 0.030$.
        \item Under Matched 1.20\% False Positives (1.49 spurious symptoms): Logistic Regression retains $0.999 \pm 0.003$ due to linear soft-margin compensation, whereas Random Forest collapses to $0.774 \pm 0.035$ (Top-1 accuracy $0.931$).
    \end{itemize}
    Crucially, the empirical effect of symptom-reporting noise is model-dependent. False-positive activations cause substantially greater degradation in Random Forest and XGBoost under the matched-error-burden comparison, whereas several other models (such as Logistic Regression and MLP) exhibit greater degradation under the tested dropout condition. Hypothesis H3 is therefore only partially supported and should not be generalized to all model architectures.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Architectural Susceptibility and the Differential Safeguard}
\label{subsec:noise_architectures}

As detailed in \cref{tab:noise_summary}, architectural resilience to reporting noise varies substantially across model paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tree Ensembles (Random Forest and XGBoost)}: Partitioning algorithms prove highly vulnerable to false-positive activations. Spurious positive indicators satisfy split predicates high in the decision trees, misrouting patient instances into incorrect terminal leaves.
    \item \textbf{Additive and Linear Models (Logistic Regression, MLP, SVM)}: Additive architectures demonstrate superior robustness to low-level spurious noise because individual false-positive features contribute bounded additive log-odds that can be outvoted by the constellation of remaining true positive symptoms.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Candidate Differential Resilience}: Crucially, even when single-label Top-1 accuracy drops under combined noise (`comb\_d10\_fp2`), \textbf{Top-3 and Top-5 diagnostic inclusion remains exceptionally resilient} ($\ge 97.6\%$ for Random Forest and $\ge 99.4\%$ for MLP and SVM), reaffirming that differential candidate ranking provides an essential buffer against catastrophic misdiagnosis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This research establishes a rigorous, leakage-aware foundation for symptom-based disease prediction:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item By forensically exposing the 93.8\% synthetic duplication and 97.6\% train-test leakage in the widely cited Kaggle benchmark, we demonstrate why prevailing na\"{i}ve 100\% accuracy claims are methodologically flawed.
    \item Through leakage-aware Duplicate-Aware Group Cross-Validation (Protocol C), we proved that partitioned tree-based models suffer substantial degradation (Decision Tree: $-17.44\%$, XGBoost: $-12.80\%$) when evaluated on genuinely unseen symptom profiles, confirming Hypothesis H1.
    \item Our leakage-aware feature selection demonstrates that a compact set of 50 consensus symptoms preserves $\ge 95\%$ retained Macro-F1 on the benchmark, identifying an algorithmically stable symptom ontology without sacrificing core predictive fidelity.
    \item Under controlled symptom sparsity simulations, we observed that while single-label Top-1 accuracy declines steeply as inputs are restricted to acute chief complaints ($s \le 3$), Top-3 and Top-5 candidate ranking retains over 88\% and 93\% inclusion on this benchmark, confirming the critical buffer provided by differential candidate ranking when patient intake profiles are incomplete.
    \item Through extensive bidirectional stochastic noise benchmarking across 20,520 cross-validation runs, we evaluated Hypothesis H3, demonstrating that noise sensitivity is model-dependent: false-positive symptom activations degrade tree-based architectures (Random Forest, XGBoost) substantially more than symptom dropouts under matched-burden controls, whereas linear and neural architectures exhibit greater degradation under dropout. Hypothesis H3 is therefore partially supported rather than universally applicable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key Findings Summary}
\label{sec:key_findings}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The Kaggle Disease Prediction dataset contains only 304 unique symptom vectors across 4,920 training records (93.8\% duplication), with 41 of 42 test records being verbatim duplicates of training records.
    \item Duplicate-Aware Group CV (Protocol C) exposes that Decision Tree accuracy collapses by $-17.44\%$ and XGBoost by $-12.80\%$ relative to standard cross-validation.
    \item A compact ontology of 50 consensus symptoms satisfies the $\ge 95\%$ retained Macro-F1 operational criterion at the dataset level, establishing algorithmic feature stability on this benchmark.
    \item Multi-candidate differential ranking (Top-3 and Top-5) consistently provides a resilient safeguard against single-label accuracy collapse under both feature reduction and simulated symptom sparsity.
    \item Stochastic noise benchmarking proves that reporting error sensitivity is model-dependent: false-positive spurious activations cause substantially steeper degradation in tree ensembles (e.g., Random Forest Macro-F1 dropping to $0.774$ under matched false positives vs. $0.911$ under matching dropout), whereas linear and neural models degrade more under symptom omission.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work and Research Roadmap}
\label{sec:future_work}

The findings of this thesis provide an empirical baseline for several immediate research extensions:

\subsection{Probability Calibration and Uncertainty Quantification (Hypothesis H4)}
\label{subsec:calibration_roadmap}
Evaluating post-hoc calibration methods (Platt scaling, Isotonic regression, and temperature scaling) to minimize Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier scores across neural and tree models, ensuring that output candidate probabilities accurately reflect diagnostic likelihoods.

\subsection{TreeSHAP Clinical Literature Concordance Audit (Hypothesis H5)}
\label{subsec:shap_audit}
Conducting an in-depth global and local feature attribution audit using TreeSHAP, systematically cross-referencing high-weight predictive symptoms against diagnostic criteria published by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

\subsection{Prospective Real-World Clinical Validation}
\label{subsec:real_world_validation}
Finally, validating the algorithmic framework on prospectively collected clinical patient cohorts featuring authentic demographic diversity, laboratory telemetry, and complex real-world comorbidities to evaluate generalizability beyond synthetic rule-derived benchmarks.
