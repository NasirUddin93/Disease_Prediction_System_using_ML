\chapter{Clinical Symptom Sparsity and Cold-Start Robustness}
\label{chap:sparsity}

While feature selection (\cref{chap:results_feature_selection}) identifies a compact global ontology for model construction, real-world deployment requires robustness against incomplete symptom presentation. In primary care and ambulatory triage encounters, patients rarely present with textbook constellations; rather, audits of clinical symptom checkers indicate that patients typically report only 3 to 8 presenting complaints~\cite{semigran2015evaluation, elias2021diagnostic, ferraccioli2022differential}. When an intake profile presents only a restricted subset of chief symptoms ($s \in [1, 10]$ active symptoms) rather than an exhaustive symptom profile (cohort natural mean: $7.45 \pm 3.59$ active symptoms, range 3--17), models must degrade gracefully. In this chapter, we evaluate this behavior through controlled stochastic masking experiments under Protocol C.

\section{Experimental Design and Stochastic Sparsity Protocol}
\label{sec:sparsity_design}
To evaluate model resilience under controlled symptom restriction on this benchmark, models were evaluated under Protocol C across restricted symptom budgets $s \in \{\text{Natural\_Unmasked}, 10, 8, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1\}$ using five independent random seeds applied across all validation records. For each validation record presenting $P$ positive symptoms, if $P > s$, a reproducible random permutation was generated to extract the prefix of length $s$, guaranteeing nested subset monotonicity ($\mathcal{S}_{s=3} \subset \mathcal{S}_{s=5}$ for each seed); if $P \le s$, all natural symptoms were preserved.

Evaluations were conducted across two representations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full 132-Symptom Space}: The comprehensive clinical input space.
    \item \textbf{Fold-Specific 50-Symptom Space}: Feature selection (Mutual Information) computed strictly on $X_{\text{train}}$ of each fold to guarantee zero validation leakage.
\end{enumerate}

Crucially, these controlled masking experiments represent a benchmark-specific synthetic stress test designed to quantify how algorithmic architectures respond to incomplete binary feature vectors on this dataset; they characterize algorithmic sensitivity rather than clinical diagnostic efficacy in real-world patient encounters.

\section{The Differential Diagnostic Safeguard Under Sparsity}
\label{sec:differential_safeguard}
As documented in Table~\ref{tab:sparsity_summary} and \cref{fig:sparsity_decay_curves}, progressive symptom restriction induces two contrasting empirical regimes across the evaluated models:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single-Label Accuracy Collapse}: Top-1 accuracy experiences a steep decline as presenting symptoms fall below $s = 5$. At $s = 3$ presenting symptoms (simulating acute reporting restriction), Top-1 accuracy declines to 75.8\% (Macro-F1: 55.1\%) for Logistic Regression, 76.7\% (Macro-F1: 57.9\%) for Random Forest, and 73.5\% (Macro-F1: 58.3\%) for MLP on the Full 132 space. At $s = 1$, Top-1 accuracy falls into the 27.8\%--50.5\% range (Macro-F1: 14.8\%--31.7\%) across all models.
    \item \textbf{Differential Diagnostic Buffer}: In sharp contrast, multi-candidate ranking demonstrates remarkable resilience. At $s = 3$ symptoms, \textbf{Top-3 diagnostic accuracy remains above 88.3\%} for Logistic Regression (88.5\%), Random Forest (89.5\%), and MLP (88.3\%), with Naive Bayes reaching 96.8\%, while \textbf{Top-5 accuracy exceeds 92.7\%} across these architectures. Even at $s = 2$ presenting symptoms, Top-5 inclusion remains between 81.7\% and 86.7\% for the leading models.
\end{enumerate}

These empirical observations highlight the vital safeguard provided by differential candidate ranking over rigid single-label classification under simulated sparsity: presenting an ordered candidate list (Top-3 to Top-5) ensures that the true disease label is captured in over 92\% of evaluated cases under moderate symptom restriction on this benchmark, providing a candidate buffer prior to confirmatory diagnostics.

\section{Architectural Sensitivity and Representation Resilience}
\label{sec:arch_sensitivity}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Additive vs.\ Partitioning Models}: Linear (Logistic Regression) and neural (MLP) architectures degrade gracefully under moderate sparsity ($s \ge 4$), as their additive decision boundaries aggregate evidence from whatever subset of features is present. Tree ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost) experience steeper drops when higher-level tree split nodes interrogate features that the patient omitted, though Random Forest maintains higher Top-1 retention at extreme sparsity ($s=1$, 50.5\%) due to bootstrap feature subsampling.
    \item \textbf{Kernel and Probabilistic Baselines}: SVM (RBF) degrades sharply at $s \le 3$ (Top-1 59.3\% at $s=3$ and 27.8\% at $s=1$) because geometric distances in the 132-dimensional kernel space are distorted by large fractions of zeroed dimensions. Conversely, Naive Bayes retains exceptionally high Top-3 and Top-5 candidate retention under moderate sparsity ($s=5$, Top-3 99.9\%), though its single-label accuracy collapses at $s=1$ (37.2\%).
    \item \textbf{Representation Generalization (132 vs.\ Fold-Specific 50 Features)}: Evaluating models trained on the fold-specific 50-symptom space revealed that retaining core consensus features achieves comparable sparsity decay trajectories to the uncompressed 132-symptom model down to $s=5$. Below $s=4$, the reduced feature pool accelerates degradation because masking active symptoms leaves fewer alternative informative features within the 50-symptom subspace.
\end{itemize}

In summary, the degradation profiles underscore that candidate differential ranking mitigates information loss when symptom reporting is incomplete on this benchmark.
